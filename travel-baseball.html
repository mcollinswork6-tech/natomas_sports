Core Philosophy

The Four Pillars

Play Bold. Stay Humble. Get Better Together.

01 Energy

Compete

Play with energy, confidence, and purpose.

Hustle on every play. Stay engaged in the dugout and on the diamond. Be aggressive within the rules. Respond to pressure by focusing on the next opportunity.

02 Growth

Improve

Get better through effort, coaching, and reflection.

Be coachable. Practice with intention. Learn from mistakes. Measure progress—not just outcomes or statistics. Development is our true scoreboard.

03 Integrity

Respect

Compete hard while treating people well.

Respect teammates, opponents, coaches, umpires, and the game. Win with humility and lose with composure. Our reputation is built on how we conduct ourselves.

04 Unity

Belong

Make the team stronger by making each other better.

Encourage teammates. Never mock, blame, exclude, or celebrate another player's failure. Every player deserves dignity and a clear, supported path to improve.

Daily Operating Standards

The Bad Hops Team Code

Five clear, memorable standards that players can repeat, remember, and put into action at every practice, scrimmage, and tournament inning.

How The Code Is Reinforced

  • ✓ Coaches model the code: in language, strategic decisions, and emotional composure.
  • ✓ Specific effort praise: players are rewarded for resilience, hustle, and sportsmanship—not only box score stats.
  • ✓ Calm correction: address mistakes consistently without humiliation or public shaming.
  • ✓ Private resolutions: repeated issues are resolved calmly and privately with player and family.
Rule 1

Next Pitch.

Mistakes happen. Bad hops happen. Strikeouts happen. Reset your mind immediately, learn from it, and lock your focus onto the next opportunity.

Rule 2

Be A Great Teammate.

Encourage others. Be loud in the dugout. No mocking, blaming, excluding, or celebrating someone else's failure—ever. We rise and fall as a unit.

Rule 3

Control What You Can Control.

Own your effort, attitude, preparation, and body language. Don't waste energy complaining about the umpire, weather, or bad bounces.

Rule 4

Be Coachable.

Listen with your eyes and ears. Ask questions, try the mechanical adjustment, and use feedback to improve instead of making excuses.

Rule 5

Respect The Game.

Play hard and fair from first pitch to final out. Show class in big wins, composure in tough losses, and respect in difficult moments.

Adult Accountability

Culture Starts With The Adults

A positive team culture is built by what adults model and what the program consistently allows. Coaches, parents, and leaders share responsibility for keeping the environment competitive, respectful, and focused on youth.

Coaches

  • • Teach, challenge, and correct without humiliation, favoritism, or personal attacks.
  • • Set clear expectations for effort, conduct, playing time, and development.
  • • Communicate decisions respectfully and address concerns in private.
  • • Model composure with umpires, opponents, players, and families.

Parents & Caregivers

  • • Support players and coaches; let the athletes own their baseball experience.
  • • Raise concerns directly through agreed communication channels.
  • • Avoid sideline confrontations, gossip, and group-chat pile-ons.
  • • Respect that development takes time and mistakes are essential to learning.

Players

  • • Follow the team code and treat teammates with dignity at all times.
  • • Listen, work hard, ask questions, and take ownership of effort and attitude.
  • • Maintain sportsmanship regardless of the scoreboard or umpire calls.
  • • Build a dugout culture where every player is engaged and supported.

Program Leaders

  • • Communicate team budgets, fees, schedules, and decisions transparently.
  • • Apply standards consistently across all players and adults.
  • • Provide a clear path for resolving concerns and disagreements.
  • • Protect player welfare and keep decisions centered on stated values.

Our Practical Conflict Standard

Address the issue, not the person. Choose a calm, private conversation rather than a public confrontation. After an emotionally charged tournament game, allow time to cool down before discussing concerns. Keep the focus on specific behavior, expectations, and constructive next steps. No retaliation, no gossip campaigns, and never using players as messengers.

Single-Age Dedication

Laser-Focused Exclusively On 11U Baseball

Rather than spreading coaching staff across multiple age brackets, Bad Hops dedicates all resources to the pivotal 11U development window. This ensures high-touch coaching, tailored reps, and rapid skill progression.

50 / 70

Intermediate Diamond

Transition to 50-foot pitching distance and 70-foot base paths, preparing players for bigger diamonds with proper arm mechanics and footwork.

Open Base

Real Baseball Rules

Lead-offs, pickoffs, pitcher balks, dropped third strike, and aggressive baserunning. We teach situational baseball IQ that translates to high school ball.

Arm Care

Strict Pitch Counts

Science-backed rest thresholds and structured warm-up throwing regimens. We prioritize long-term player health over short-term weekend wins.

11–12 Cap

Tight Roster Philosophy

We intentionally cap team rosters at 11–12 dedicated players so every athlete receives meaningful defensive innings, quality at-bats, and game experience.
